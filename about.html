About

I’m Matt. I write music, and lately it’s been misbehaving.

What I’m after

I’ve been fascinated recently, in my own writing, by music that transgresses some aspect of the accepted Western ontologies of genre and musical performance. Not for transgression’s sake in and of itself; it’s simply a thread I’ve noticed in my recent work.

My doctoral dissertation, much to the bewilderment of my dissertation committee, was a piece that cannot physically be performed as written (and an explanation of how or why is intentionally never given). My next substantial work after that was an instrumental/vocal chamber piece which is not meant to be listened to, at least not by any external audience.

On concerts

I have less and less interest for the concert hall, that 19th-century museum full of mummified corpses.

Music to me is not an abstraction. It’s like a speech act; it’s meant to do something.

That’s a big part of why the scores on this site are free. If music is meant to do something, it has to be out in the world where people can pick it up and use it. Here’s what you can do with it.

Background

I’ve been writing music since 2004. I studied composition at Westminster Choir College with Joel Phillips and at Carnegie Mellon with Leonardo Balada, orchestral conducting at the University of South Carolina with Donald Portnoy, and composition again at the University of Maryland with David Froom.

Elsewhere
