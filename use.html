Using the music

What you can do with the music

Short answer: a lot, and mostly without asking me. Everything on this site is released under [Creative Commons license: not chosen yet]. Here’s what that means in practice.

Choose the license first. Several answers on this page depend on it: whether it’s NonCommercial (NC), whether it’s ShareAlike (SA), and that it isn’t NoDerivatives (ND), because ND would rule out arrangements. Once it’s chosen, set it in assets/js/site.js and it fills in across the site. Then fill in each highlighted spot below. I haven’t written any terms you haven’t decided.

How

01

Performing it

You don’t need my permission to perform anything here. Download the PDF, print as many copies as your group needs, and go. That goes for choirs, schools, churches, recitals, readings and run-throughs.

Paid and ticketed concerts: say whether they’re fine as-is. If the license is NonCommercial, spell out here what counts as commercial for a performance.

Please print from the PDF on this site rather than photocopying someone else’s copy. That way everyone has the latest version, with any corrections.

Performing-rights organisation: if you’re a member of one (ASCAP, BMI, SOCAN, PRS and so on), say so here and explain what a presenter should do, for example list the piece in their returns. If you’re not, say that instead.

If you perform something of mine, I’d genuinely like to hear about it. Tell me.

02

Recording it

You can record the music and share the recording: a concert video, a livestream, an album, a track on a streaming service.

Recording terms: whether releasing a recording for sale or on streaming services is fine as-is, and anything you want people to do when they release one.

Distributors: some ask for a “mechanical licence” or proof of permission before they’ll release a recording of someone else’s composition. Decide what you want people to show them (a link to this page and the license, a note from you, or something else).

Film, games, adverts and other synced uses: say whether these are covered or whether people should ask first.

03

Arranging it

Make your own versions: a different voicing, a new instrumentation, a reduction, a simpler part for a student, a version for whatever ensemble you actually have.

ShareAlike: if the license is SA, explain that arrangements must be shared under the same license. If it isn’t, say whether arrangers can license their arrangement however they like.

Put my name on it as the composer and yours as the arranger. There’s a line for that below.

Do you want people to send you their arrangements? Would you link to them, or host them here?

Worth knowing when you choose: the Creative Commons 4.0 licenses already have a royalties clause (section 2(b)(3) of the legal code). In short, the licensor waives royalties for the uses the license allows, wherever that waiver is possible. The NonCommercial versions keep the right to collect for commercial uses. Whatever you write here has to line up with that.

05

Crediting me

Every Creative Commons license asks for credit. A good credit names the piece, the composer, where it came from and the license. Copy whichever of these fits and change the details.

Draft wording. Check you’re happy with these formats.

In a concert program

Title of the Piece (Year) · Matthew Samson Score: matthewsamson.com · [license]

With a recording, video or stream

“Title of the Piece” by Matthew Samson. Score from matthewsamson.com, used under [license].

On an arrangement

Title of the Piece Music by Matthew Samson, arranged by Your Name. Original score from matthewsamson.com, used under [license].

Each piece’s page in the reader shows its own credit line too, so you can copy the exact title from there.

06

When to ask me

If you want to do something the license doesn’t cover, or you’re just not sure, ask. Email me. I’d rather you ask than not do it.
